# Definition

The **Kettle Process** is Hearth & Wheel's overnight sourdough routine. Starter is fed and the dough is left in a proving cabinet humidified by a steam kettle for roughly **14 hours**, after which it is shaped and baked in the wood-fired oven.

A batch that is "in the Kettle Process" is **still proving and not yet ready for the oven**. Handover notes and staff talk use the phrase this way, e.g. "the rye batch is still in the Kettle Process".

# Stages

1. Starter is fed.
2. Dough is placed in the steam-kettle-humidified proving cabinet.
3. It proves for roughly 14 hours (overnight).
4. It is shaped and baked. At this point it has left the Kettle Process.

# Why it matters downstream

Dough that is under- or over-proved bakes unevenly. That shows up as a spike in the [Ember Index](/bakehouse/ember-index.md), the overnight oven-stability score, and can lead to spoiled or scorched batches.

# Related
